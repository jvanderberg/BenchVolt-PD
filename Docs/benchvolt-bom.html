Key facts

  • MCU is STM32F070RBT6 (LQFP64), 128 KiB flash / 16 KiB RAM. (Earlier local notes said "CB"; schematic says RB.)
  • USB PD negotiation done by STUSB4500QTR on bit-banged I²C at 0x28; MCU supervises only (F070 has no UCPD).
  • BuckBoost sheet is instantiated 3× (DC1, DC2, 0.8–22V rail) — quantities below marked ×3; the schematic only shows one refdes set.
  • DC1 programmed to 3.0 V (feeds 1.8/2.5 V LDOs), DC2 to 5.5 V (feeds 3.3 V LDO and 0.5–5.5 V MIC69502) — both C firmware (main.c) and Rust (power.rs rail_setpoint). Third TPS55289 runs from VBUS_SINK directly → 0.8–22 V CH5.
  • Display: ST7789 controller, 170×320 panel (firmware src/startup.rs: Builder::st7789 ... with_display_size(170, 320), color inverted → IPS). Generic ~1.9" IPS SPI TFT module, 8-pin 2.54mm header J3 (GND,VCC,SCL,SDA,RES,DC,CS,BLK). SPI at 24 MHz; full frame ~106 KB > 16 KB RAM so no framebuffer.
  • "LM29302R" is real: HTC Korea / Taejin LM29302R (LCSC C481680), 3A adj LDO TO-263-5, MIC29302 second-source, same pinout. Alternates: Microchip MIC29302AWU-TR ($1.83 / $1.11), UMW MIC29302WU ($0.86 / $0.53, 45k in stock).
  • Rotary encoder is not on the schematic as a part; connects via J5 (A/B/C with C61–C63 debounce caps).
  • Retail: $179 on Crowd Supply (confirmed 2026-09-27).

Priced BOM (qty 1 vs qty 100)

I priced the BenchVolt-PD board at about $74 per board at quantity 1 and $52 per board at quantity 100. That covers parts, the display and a bare 4-layer PCB, but not assembly or the enclosure. The ICs and passives were looked up on LCSC through JLCPCB's parts search on 2026-09-27. The display, PCB, banana jacks, headers and shunt resistors are estimates.

DigiKey and Mouser mostly blocked automated fetching, so the table uses LCSC prices. The two DigiKey qty-1 prices I did get (2026-09-27): STM32F070RBT6 $4.59 (LCSC $2.04) and MIC69502WR $3.82 (LCSC $3.76). The ICs would cost roughly 1.3–2× more from DigiKey or Mouser.

"LM29302R" is a real part number: HTC Korea / Taejin LM29302R (LCSC C481680). It is a 3A adjustable LDO in TO-263-5, a second-source of Microchip's MIC29302 with the same pinout. You can swap in the Microchip MIC29302AWU-TR ($1.83 / $1.11) or the UMW MIC29302WU ($0.86 / $0.53, 45k in stock).

About 60% of the parts cost is three components: the three TPS55289 buck-boosts (~$15.60), the 24 × 10µF/50V 1206 MLCCs (~$8.40, 12¢ more each than any smaller cap) and the USB-C connector.

Assumptions and caveats

  • Qty-1 small-part pricing: at quantity 1, LCSC's minimum-order strips make the cheap parts cost more in practice. Resistors come in strips of 100 per value, which adds roughly $8–10 of extra stock to a one-off build.
  • Qty-100 column: it uses each part's 100-piece price. For 100 boards you'd buy 300–2,400 of many parts, so the real figure would come out slightly lower.
  • PCB: size is inferred from the aluminium base in Enclosure/ (162×77 mm). The $5 at qty 1 is JLCPCB's typical 5-board 4-layer order (about $20–30) divided by 5; a single board still costs the whole order. About $2.50 per board at 100 is an estimate.
  • Display: TZT's store lists the 8-pin module at $1.80, and AliExpress listings run about $3–4; I used $3 and $2.
  • Banana jacks: generic 4 mm binding posts. Pomona safety jacks would add about $15–25 per board.
  • Thin stock at LCSC: STL6P3LLH6 (1 in stock), EEHZA1H680P (1), MIC69502WR (0; the -TR version is $7.59) and LM29302R (8). A clone or second source may be needed.
  • Inferred values: the tantalum value (10µF/35V) and the resistor/shunt packages are not stated on the schematic.

Assembly (estimate, not in the totals): at JLCPCB, a batch of 5 boards would cost about $25 in setup and stencil plus about $3 for each unique extended part. With about 40 of those, that's roughly $30 per board. At 100 boards it drops to about $3–5 per board. Hand-soldering the through-hole parts is extra.

Excluded:

  • Enclosure: 5 laser-cut 3 mm acrylic panels plus a machined 162×77×10 mm aluminium base, from the STL files. Rough guess: about $40–70 at qty 1 and about $12–20 at 100.
  • Rotary encoder and knob: the README mentions an encoder, but it isn't on the schematic. A Bourns PEC11R is about $2.70 / $1.67 on LCSC, a generic EC11 about $0.30, and a knob about $0.50.
  • Shipping, duties and tariffs.
